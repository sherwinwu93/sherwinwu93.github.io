26 Your Officer

They shoot at me with weapons and words, and I fire back with courage and compassion.
It is my duty to turn chaos into composure, pandemonium into peace,
  not only with my muscle but with my mind.
I experience life and death and cry tears of joy and pain all at the same time.
The lessons of love and despair I have practiced since birth have prepared me for today.
I end my shift with more scars than when I began, some on my body, some in my heart.
There are comrades I have buried and many I have carried, ones who held my hand from the start.
I am here for your comfort, to serve and protect you,
  to catch the burden of displaced evil and deflect harm.
I bleed, I feel, I care, and I am real.
I am your officer.
Jenny Christoforakis, Chicago police officer.

sentence

shoot at sb.: 充某人开枪

  • You should've shot at him when you were not sure that he died.
  • Don't shoot at my cat, who has accompanied me since my birth.
  • Our comrade can't resist the temptation to shoot at people with bare hands.

fire back with sth.: 用某物还击

  • I should've fired back with the mistakes she made in the university.
  • Wrapping up the dog shit, she fired back with the parcel.
  • Firing back with anger, he threw a stone at his classmate, who took off his pants in public.

It is my duty to do sth.: 做某事是我的义务

  • It is my duty to tell you what has happened when you're not here.
  • It is our duty to offer the truth to the stupid as a journalist.
  • Serving as a staff member, it is my duty to finish my work on time.

composure [kəmˈpoʊʒər] n.: the state of feeling or seeming calm镇静

  • If I find it hard to keep my composure, I will stop making any decision.
  • There was some composure on the board when we found some wreck in the salvage operation.
  • I couldn't keep my composure when I learned that some pirates had sacked our village.

pandemonium [ˌpændəˈmoʊniəm] n.: a situation in which there is a lot of noise because people are angry, confused or frightened SYN chaos喧嚣；大混乱

  • The so-called justice march ended up a massive pandemonium.
  • It's your duty to settle down the pandemonium as a policeman.
  • In the pandemonium, the lord and several of his knights had their heads cut.

scar [skɑr] n.: a permanent mark that is left on your skin after you have had a cut or wound疤痕

  • No wonder he has been wearing a hat all day, for he was hiding his scar.
  • Because of his scar on his face, he lost a large number of opportunities to get a good job.
  • The crime he committed is why there's a scar on his face.

comrade [ˈkɑmˌræd] n.: formal a friend, especially someone who shares difficult work or danger同志,伙伴

  • It is common sense that civil servants are called comrades in China.
  • With a car with the red flag coming into the camp, the comrade stood to attention.
  • He should be regarded as our comrade, not a useless tool.

displaced [dɪsˈpleɪs] adj.: 转移的

  • The displaced treasure must have been found.
  • The pirate takes no interest in anything but the displaced treasure.
  • No one could account for the fact that the displaced treasure has been found by a sheep stealer.

catch the burden of sth.: 承担某事的重担

  • In the end, she gave up catching the burden of raising up her sister.
  • She was asked to catch the burden of settling down the queen who just lost her son.
  • She caught the burden of bringing up all of her siblings.

deflect [dɪˈflekt] v.: if someone or something deflects something that is moving, or if it deflects, it turns in a different direction抵挡

  • We must have knocked him to the ground to deflect his crime.
  • If we failed to deflect our enemies, our wives and children would have been raped.
  • She makes an attempt to deflect the wild dragon.

CN

他们用武器和言语向我射击，而我用勇气与慈悲还击。

我的职责，是将混乱化为镇定，将喧嚣与骚乱化为和平，
不仅凭借我的肌肉，也凭借我的头脑。

我经历着生与死，也在同一时刻流下喜悦与痛苦的泪水。
从出生起，我就在经历和练习着爱与绝望的教训，而这一切都让我为今天做好了准备。

结束一天的工作时，我身上的伤疤比开始时更多——
有些留在身体上，有些留在心里。

我埋葬过一些并肩作战的战友，也背负、搀扶过许多人；
他们中的一些人，从一开始就握着我的手。

我在这里，是为了给你安慰，为了服务你、保护你，
为了接住被转嫁而来的邪恶所带来的重负，并挡开伤害。

我会流血，我会感受，我会在乎，
而且我是真实存在的。

我是你的警官。

——Jenny Christoforakis，芝加哥警察
